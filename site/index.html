The script

Malayalam is an abugida written left to right. A consonant carries an inherent vowel; other vowels attach as marks before, after, above or below the consonant, and the virama U+0D4D either suppresses the vowel or joins two consonants into a conjunct. Conjuncts are the reason coverage alone tells you nothing: സ്ത്രീ is six codepoints that must resolve to one cluster.

A 1971 orthographic reform replaced many stacked conjuncts with separated forms and introduced the chillu letters — ൻ and its five siblings, consonants with no inherent vowel, encoded atomically since Unicode 5.1. Both orthographies remain in use, so a family is judged against the older forms too.

Shaping runs through the Universal Shaping Engine, which reads the mlm2 script tag. The older mlym tag is still accepted by some stacks and ignored by others, which is where identical-looking fonts diverge between applications.

Of the 61 indexed families that cover the block, 19 shape the exemplar sets cleanly, 15 shape with caveats and 27 break. Filter the specimen list below by verdict.

Orthography and encoding notes for Malayalam at r12a.io ↗ — external

Two encoding problems most verdicts turn on

ൻ Chillu letters, encoded twice

Before Unicode 5.1 a chillu was written as consonant + virama + ZWJ — 0D28 0D4D 200D for chillu n. Unicode 5.1 encoded the chillus atomically at U+0D7A–0D7F, and the older sequences stayed valid for backward compatibility. Two representations of the same letter now circulate: text typed on one keyboard does not match text typed on another, search misses words that differ only by ZWJ, and a font that builds only one form renders the other as a consonant with a visible virama — a different word, not a different style. അവൻ and അവന് mean different things.

ൻ്റ The conjunct ṉṯa, encoded three ways

Unicode recommends chillu n + virama + rra — 0D7B 0D4D 0D31 — but na + virama + rra 0D28 0D4D 0D31 was already in wide use and remains what InScript keyboards and most transliteration tools produce. A third sequence inserts ZWJ, and that one collides with the legacy encoding of the side-by-side form ൻറ, so the same codepoints mean two different glyphs depending on what the implementation assumes. All three are widespread. A family that builds only the recommended sequence will show the other two as separated letters, which is why this conjunct decides more verdicts on this page than any other.

What failure looks like · loud and quiet

0D4E 0D15 · dot reph + ka
ൎക
rule present · Noto Sans Malayalam
◌ൎക
rule absent · dotted circle

The dot reph is a prescribed mark, not a spacing letter. With no rule to attach it, the shaper inserts U+25CC as a base and the mark lands on a dotted circle. Loud failure, easy to spot.

0D28 0D4D 0D31 · na, virama, rra — and 0D7B 0D4D 0D31 · chillu n, virama, rra
ന്റ
conjunct formed
ൻറ
rule missing · letters left apart

Both sequences should give the conjunct ൻ്റ. A font that builds only one of them draws the other as chillu n beside rra — no dotted circle, no error, a different word. Quiet failure, and why coverage checks and dotted-circle checks are both insufficient.

An exemplar set is the list of characters a language actually writes with, published per language in SLDR. It is narrower than the script and never identical to it: two languages sharing Malayalam can require different characters. Glyph Sleuth shapes every sequence in a language's exemplar set through a font and calls the font a fit only if all of them come out clean.

Four of the seventeen languages written in this script have no font that passes. Paniya pcg and Ravula yea are two of them. Every character they need is encoded and covered by indexed fonts; no indexed font shapes their combinations correctly.
